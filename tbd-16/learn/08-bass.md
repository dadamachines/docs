## Planned outline

- **Track 9 is Bass**, and it runs the **TBD03** — a 303-style acid voice.
- The thing to notice: TBD03 puts the **filter on page 1**, before the oscillator. That ordering is the lesson. On this instrument the filter *is* the sound.

| Page 1 knob | What it does |
|:--|:--|
| `Cutoff` | How open the filter is — the single most important knob on the machine |
| `Reso` | How much the filter emphasises its own cutoff point — the "squelch" |
| `Env Amt` | How far each note kicks the cutoff upward |
| `VCF Dec` | How fast that kick falls back down |
{: .dada-minimal-table }

- Why those four together make an acid line: every note opens the filter and it closes again, so a line of identical notes still moves.
- Writing a bassline that works with the kick: notes on and around the kick, space where the kick isn't.
- **Accent** and **Slide** — page 3 and page 4. Slide is what makes consecutive notes glide rather than restart, and it's most of the 303 sound.
- Choosing notes: root note on the downbeat, octave jumps for energy, a fifth for tension. Starting with three notes is enough.
- Track 10 is a second Bass track running the same machine — one for the sub, one for the acid line.

The finished exercise will provide a short editable TBD03 line and the four
filter controls above, so the learner can hear the acid movement directly.
