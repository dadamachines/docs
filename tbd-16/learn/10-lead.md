# Lead sounds

The one place on the device where browsing presets is genuinely the fastest way to work.
{: .fs-6 .fw-300 }

{% include tbd-learn-progress.html lesson="10" stage="Stage C — add notes" status="planned" status_label="Planned" %}

**You now have:** drums, a bassline, and the ability to play parts in live.

{: .note }
> This lesson is being written. The outline below is what it will cover.

## Planned outline

- **Track 11 is Lead**, running the **Mono Synth** — and unlike the drum tracks it ships with real preset variety:

| Preset | Use it for |
|:--|:--|
| `Default` | A clean starting point |
| `Bass` | A round sub, if you want a second bass rather than a lead |
| `Acid` | A brighter, more aggressive line |
| `Pluck` | Short and percussive — good for arpeggios and stabs |
| `Drone` | Long and evolving, for pads and texture |
{: .dada-minimal-table }

- Page 1 of the Mono Synth is `Shape`, `Timbre`, `Color`, `Warp` — a different four from the drums, because this is a *modelled* oscillator rather than a drum voice. Turning `Shape` moves between whole synthesis models.
- `SR Red` on page 2 is a sample-rate reducer — the quickest route to a lo-fi lead.
- **Track 12 is Lead2**, running the **Wavetable Osc** with 32 factory wavetables, plus a proper ADSR envelope on page 3. Very different flavour, same role.
- Writing a lead that doesn't fight the bass: leave the low end alone, play in the gaps the drums leave, use fewer notes than you think.
- Getting an arpeggio without playing one: the per-pattern arpeggiator on the Track Parameters screen — direction, steps, distance, speed and gate.

The finished exercise will let the learner audition the small preset set,
choose a starting sound, play a few notes, and shape it with the four visible
parameters.
