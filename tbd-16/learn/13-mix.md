# Space and mix

Everything is playing at once and it sounds like mud. Here's how to fix that.
{: .fs-6 .fw-300 }

{% include tbd-learn-progress.html lesson="13" stage="Stage D — make it a track" status="planned" status_label="Planned" %}

**You now have:** a lot of parts competing for the same space.

{: .note }
> This lesson is being written. The outline below is what it will cover.

## Planned outline

- **Level first.** Nothing else in a mix matters until the relative volumes are right. Start with everything down, bring the kick up to comfortable, then add each part until you can just hear it.
- **Pan** — you have two ears; use them. Hats slightly off-centre, percussion opposite, kick and bass dead centre where they belong.
- **Filter** on the track mixer: rolling the low end off everything that isn't the kick or bass is the single biggest clean-up move available.
- **The two send effects**, on their own tracks:
  - **FX1 — Delay.** `Time`, `Sync`, `Fdbk`, `Freeze`. Synced delay for rhythm, free-running for texture, `Freeze` for a performance move.
  - **FX2 — Reverb.** `Time`, `Damp`, `Diffuse`, `HP`. The `HP` control matters more than it looks — it keeps reverb out of the low end, which is where mud comes from.
- Sends versus insert effects: why one reverb shared by everything sounds more like a room than five separate ones.
- **Mixer groups** — four groups with their own mute and their own level, pan and FX offsets. Put all the drums in G1 and you can duck the entire kit with one control.
- **Master** — output volume, and a compressor (`Thresh`, `Ratio`, `Attack`, `Release`, `MakeUp`, `Mix`) to glue the whole thing. The `Hot` master preset is a louder starting point.
- When *not* to reach for effects: usually the answer is that two parts are playing in the same register and one of them should move.

The finished exercise will start with an intentionally muddy four-part mix and
let the learner improve level, pan, filtering, and send effects while it loops.
