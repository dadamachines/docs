The 16 buttons are also a keyboard. Stop drawing notes and start playing them.

## Planned outline

- The **REC** button cycles three modes, shown in the display header:

| Mode | Header | The 16 buttons become |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Step** | `Stp` | A grid — press a button to switch a step on or off |
| **Play** | `Ply` | An instrument — drum pads on drum tracks, a keyboard on synth tracks |
| **Record** | `Rec` | The same instrument, but what you play is captured into the pattern |
{: .dada-minimal-table }

- **The keyboard layout on a synth track:** the bottom row is the white keys (C D E F G A B C), the top row holds the black keys, and **step 1** and **step 8** shift the octave down and up.
- **On a drum track**, buttons 9–16 trigger drum tracks 1–8 — so you can finger-drum the whole kit from one screen.
- Why live recording sounds different from step programming: your timing is imperfect, and imperfect is what makes it feel human. You can always fix individual steps afterwards in the Step Editor.
- Quantising after the fact, and when not to.
- Recording over a loop that's already playing — layering rather than starting over.

The finished exercise will switch the virtual buttons between step grid,
playable keyboard, and live recording, then let the learner compare the
recorded timing with a quantised version.
